## Concept & Mathematical Foundation

Discovered independently by mathematician Robert Ammann and physicist F. P. M. Beenker in 1982, the **Ammann–Beenker tiling** is an aperiodic tessellation of the Euclidean plane composed of $45^\circ$ rhombuses and squares. It possesses exact global 8-fold rotational symmetry (the signature of octagonal quasicrystals) and exhibits an irrational tile frequency ratio governed by the silver ratio:

$$\delta_S = 1 + \sqrt{2} \approx 2.41421356...$$

Unlike periodic crystal lattices whose translations leave the structure unchanged, quasicrystals possess an additional internal degree of freedom known in solid-state physics as **phasons**. While phonons correspond to ordinary sound waves and physical displacements, phason fluctuations represent collective topological reconfigurations: local hexagonal clusters of two squares and two rhombuses dynamically interchange their internal diagonals and glide along continuous aperiodic "worm" corridors.

This artwork implements N. G. de Bruijn's dual multigrid method, projecting a 4-dimensional hypercubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^4$ onto the 2D plane through four equidistant line families oriented at angles $k\pi/4$ ($k=0,1,2,3$). As a continuous harmonic orbit traverses the 4D phason subspace, the tiles smoothly breathe, inflate, deflate, and undergo seamless Conway–Radin tile flips, returning with absolute mathematical precision to their initial configuration at $T = 720$ frames for a perfect 12.0s seamless loop.
